PROJECT BANNER — DESIGN DECISIONS
Updated: 2026-10-10
Status: Working design notes. Q1–Q113 baseline preserved; follow-up decisions appended below.
Source note: the original Q114 map-knowledge prompt and newer Q111–Q119 prompts use overlapping question numbers. Use topic labels, not numbers, to match decisions.

CORE VISION
- Sandbox medieval/low-fantasy world with no main quest, chosen one, or forced story.
- Shared, synchronized persistent world, with detailed local simulation and abstract distant simulation.
- Finite procedural geography, east-west wrapping contemplated; first discovery establishes canonical places for later players.
- The world exists as simulation before it exists as scenery. Geography, resources, climate, history and events drive settlements and cultures.
- Offline characters/companies continue to exist and face real risks; safe locations, guards and standing orders mitigate them.
- Player encounters are rare in a huge world, with player-created hubs and temporary hosted interactions.
- Player can live as trader, mercenary, noble, criminal, explorer, ruler, pirate, etc.

EARLIER LOCKED DECISIONS (Q1–Q80, summarized)
- Multiple character-start presets plus custom starts. Death permits succession to an heir/companion; no successor means player game over, not world reset.
- Characters age with shared world time, including offline. Global time ratio TBD.
- Optional deep family system: marriage, children, education, wills, succession. Player-parent first child control rolled, second child goes to other parent. Child mortality possible; playable at 16. Siblings live independently. Betrayal rare and causally justified.
- NPCs have personalities, motives, imperfect memories, relationships and capacity to lie. Rumors, spies, evidence, counterintelligence and disguises exist.
- Imprisonment, escape, ransom, prison politics and limited outside operations exist.
- Crime, fronts, investigation, corruption, law enforcement, governance, political factions and coups are simulated.
- Player-created laws have economic/social consequences. Governments can be delegated to officials, governors and institutions; corruption can be investigated.
- Religion has institutions and player-founded/reformed faiths, but doctrine effects are relatively lightweight. Languages mostly cultural flavor.
- Disease is relatively uncommon and simple. No major natural disasters. Weather and seasons affect travel, agriculture, trade and battle. Winter travel can be dangerous.
- Roads, bridges, tolls, dynamic routes and settlement founding/growth/decline/ruins are systemic. Players place functional buildings; settlements expand physically and respond to economic geography.
- Supply/demand drives trade. Monopolies, regional currencies, simple inflation, loans/collateral and risk-based insurance exist.
- Ships support crews, cargo, fleets, combat, upgrades, captains and automated trade. No manual sailing; naval battles mostly auto-resolve, with optional command/boarding. Captured ships, piracy and basic privateering licenses supported.
- Bounties can be issued by governments/players; official ones require cause. Live capture depends on contract. Surrender can be negotiated before/during battle.

FOLLOW-UP DECISIONS (2026-10-10; later chat, separately numbered)
- Living-world simulation: D — deep, independently evolving world; politics, economics, warfare, migration, religion, population and other connected systems continue without player intervention. Extends existing core vision; no new implementation task implied.
- World knowledge and intelligence: B + C — knowledge is gained through exploration and contacts. Scouts, spies, merchants and travellers can deliver incomplete, outdated, inaccurate or deliberately misleading reports. Does NOT decide whether formal maps can be purchased/shared or whether cartography is a separate profession.
- Simulation level-of-detail: D — detailed nearby simulation, abstract simulation at distance, with importance-aware detail to preserve believable outcomes and performance. Restates pre-existing core vision; avoid re-asking.
- Settlement and kingdom administration: D — rich population, economic, construction, defence, law, tax, politics, infrastructure and diplomacy management, with optional NPC delegation/automation. Delegates have capabilities, loyalties and personal motives that can affect outcomes.
- Campaign format: B — open-ended sandbox with player-chosen objectives; no mandatory campaign story, quests or forced ending. This reaffirms original core vision.
- Official modding support: A — none planned. Do not allocate resources to an official editor or modding ecosystem; this does not imply actively blocking unofficial modifications.
- Sound and atmosphere: C — cinematic and reactive audio, including dynamic music, dramatic battles, character voices and reactive soundscapes. This is a design direction, not a claim that audio assets are implemented.
- Difficulty and realism: C — historically grounded, challenging systems with meaningful consequences, while avoiding gratuitous punishment or repetitive micromanagement. This does not select an elaborate player-configurable realism menu.
- Multiplayer: already addressed in earlier decisions (shared persistent world; offline continuation; sparse encounters; player hubs and temporarily hosted player interactions). Later multiplayer multiple-choice prompt was identified as a duplicate and NOT answered; do not interpret it as a new decision or a mandate for MMO/always-online operation.

DESIGN QUESTION PROTOCOL (user preference, 2026-10-10)
- Ask ONE broad multiple-choice question at a time, with A/B/C/D options.
- Each question should cover an entire genuinely unresolved system; avoid tiny mechanical questions and unnecessary follow-ups.
- Check this register first to avoid re-asking settled questions. Merge choices such as B+C when specified.
- Use explicit topic titles for new decisions; question numbers from different chats overlap and are not global identifiers.
- These notes document intent, NOT permission for Hermes or anyone else to interrupt running work, alter active branches, merge, execute code, schedule tasks, or change automation.
